LAB1

Phishing Email Identification

Examine 10 sample emails and classify each as Safe, Phishing, or Unsure — then identify the specific red flags present.

⏱ 20 minutes Intermediate
🎯

Learning Objective

By the end of this lab, you will be able to distinguish phishing emails from legitimate ones by analysing sender addresses, content tone, link structures, and emotional manipulation tactics.

✅ Success Criteria: Correctly classify at least 8 out of 10 emails. Identify at least 3 red flags per phishing email.
📋

What You Will Analyse

  • Sender email address & domain
  • Subject line urgency or unusual phrasing
  • Greeting — personalised or generic?
  • Link URLs (hover to preview destination)
  • Spelling & grammar errors
  • Emotional trigger used (urgency/fear/curiosity)
  • Requests for credentials or personal info
  • Attachment type and filename
📌

Step-by-Step Instructions

  • 1
    Read each email below completely
    Read the full email — subject, sender, body, and any links or attachments mentioned. Do not skim.
  • 2
    Assign a verdict: SAFE / PHISHING / UNSURE
    Use the verdict column in your worksheet or mentally note your decision before looking at the answer key.
  • 3
    List at least 3 red flags for each phishing email
    Write down specific evidence — e.g., "sender domain is 'paypa1.com' not 'paypal.com'" or "uses generic greeting 'Dear User'".
  • 4
    Identify the manipulation tactic used
    Classify the psychological trigger: Urgency / Fear / Authority / Curiosity / Familiarity / Scarcity.
  • 5
    Compare with the answer key and discuss
    Use the "Reveal Answer" buttons below. Discuss any you got wrong with your group — understanding mistakes is more valuable than just getting them right.

📧 Sample Emails — Classify Each One

From:security-alert@paypa1.com To:you@yourcompany.com
⚠️ URGENT: Your PayPal Account Has Been Limited — Action Required

Click below to restore access: http://paypal-account-verify.secure-login.net/restore Failure to verify will result in immediate account closure. PayPal Security Team
🚩 Fake sender domain 🚩 Urgency tactic 🚩 Suspicious link 🚩 Generic greeting
VERDICT: PHISHING

Red Flags:
1. Sender domain is paypa1.com (number "1" instead of letter "l") — typosquatting
2. Urgency: "within 24 hours" / "immediate account closure"
3. Link goes to paypal-account-verify.secure-login.net — NOT paypal.com
4. Generic greeting "Dear Customer" — PayPal always uses your name
5. Fear tactic: threat of permanent suspension
Manipulation Tactic: URGENCY + FEAR
From:noreply@github.com To:you@yourcompany.com
[GitHub] Please verify your email address
Hi John, Please verify your GitHub email address by clicking the link below: https://github.com/users/john/email_verify?token=abc123def456 This link expires in 24 hours. If you did not create a GitHub account, you can ignore this email. The GitHub Team
✓ Legitimate sender ✓ Official GitHub link ✓ Personalised greeting
VERDICT: SAFE

Why it's safe:
1. Sender is noreply@github.com — official GitHub domain
2. Link goes directly to github.com — no redirects
3. Uses personalised greeting with your name
4. Provides a safe "ignore this email" option for unrecognised accounts
5. No aggressive urgency or threats
From:ahmed.ceo@yourcompany-secure.com To:you@yourcompany.com
Confidential — Urgent Bank Transfer Needed
Hi, I'm currently in a board meeting and can't take calls. I need you to process a wire transfer of $15,000 to our vendor before EOD today — it's urgent for a deal closing. Please transfer to: Account: 8847291054 Bank: First Digital Bank Routing: 021000021 Reply back when done. Do not discuss this with anyone as it's confidential. Ahmed Khan CEO, YourCompany
🚩 Fake CEO domain 🚩 Wire transfer request 🚩 Authority + Secrecy
VERDICT: PHISHING (BEC — Business Email Compromise)

Red Flags:
1. Sender domain: yourcompany-secure.com — NOT yourcompany.com
2. Requests financial transaction via email — organisations never do this
3. "Do not discuss with anyone" — classic secrecy tactic to prevent verification
4. "Can't take calls" — prevents phone verification
5. Impersonating a high-authority figure (CEO)
6. Unusual urgency: "before EOD today"
Manipulation Tactic: AUTHORITY + URGENCY + SECRECY
ACTION: Never process financial requests via email. Call the CEO directly on their known phone number.
From:it-helpdesk@yourcompany.it-support-portal.com To:all-staff@yourcompany.com
IT NOTICE: Your Password Expires in 2 Hours — Reset Now
Dear Employee, Your corporate password will expire in 2 hours. Please reset it immediately using the link below to avoid being locked out of all company systems. Reset Password Now: https://yourcompany.it-support-portal.com/reset?id=49182 This is an automated message from the IT Department. — IT Security Team
🚩 Subdomain trick 🚩 2-hour pressure 🚩 Sent to all-staff
VERDICT: PHISHING

Red Flags:
1. Sender domain: yourcompany.it-support-portal.com — the real domain is yourcompany.com. Here yourcompany is just a SUBDOMAIN of the attacker's it-support-portal.com
2. Extreme urgency: "2 hours"
3. Threatening consequence: "locked out of all systems"
4. Sent to all-staff — mass phishing blast
5. Link domain confirms the subdomain trick
Manipulation Tactic: URGENCY + FEAR
From:billing@microsoft.com To:you@yourcompany.com
Your Microsoft 365 subscription has been renewed
Hi John Smith, Your Microsoft 365 Business Standard subscription was renewed on 20 May 2025. Amount charged: $12.50 to Visa ending in 4521 Next renewal: 20 May 2026 View your subscription at: https://admin.microsoft.com/account/billing If you have any questions, visit our support page at https://support.microsoft.com Microsoft Billing Team
✓ Official MS domain ✓ Personalised name ✓ Known account details
VERDICT: SAFE

Why it's safe:
1. Sender billing@microsoft.com — official Microsoft domain
2. Uses full personalised name — not "Dear Customer"
3. Includes partial card details (last 4 digits) showing knowledge of real account
4. Both links go to official microsoft.com and support.microsoft.com
5. No threats, no urgency, informational only
From:delivery-noreply@dhl-parcel-track.net To:you@yourcompany.com
DHL: Your parcel could not be delivered — action required
Dear Customer, We attempted to deliver your parcel #GH48291 today but were unable to complete delivery due to an incomplete address. To reschedule your delivery, please pay a small redelivery fee of £1.50: Reschedule here: http://bit.ly/dhl-redeliver-uk Note: If not rescheduled within 48 hours, the parcel will be returned to sender. DHL Express UK
🚩 Fake DHL domain 🚩 URL shortener 🚩 Payment request
VERDICT: PHISHING

Red Flags:
1. Sender domain: dhl-parcel-track.net — NOT dhl.com
2. URL shortener (bit.ly) hiding the real destination
3. Requesting payment via email — DHL never requests payment this way
4. Generic tracking number — could be sent to millions
5. Generic greeting "Dear Customer"
Manipulation Tactic: CURIOSITY + URGENCY
NOTE: This is also a common SMS phishing (smishing) attack format.

LAB2

URL & Domain Analysis

Examine 15 URLs to classify them as Legitimate, Suspicious, or Malicious. Use VirusTotal to scan live URLs and understand attack techniques.

⏱ 15 minutes Intermediate
🔡

Typosquatting

Registering misspelled versions of real domains. E.g., amaz0n.com instead of amazon.com. Relies on users not noticing character substitutions.
🌐

Subdomain Abuse

Making a real brand name appear as a subdomain. The REAL domain is what comes just before .com/.net/.org. In paypal.evil-site.com, the domain is evil-site.com, not PayPal.
🔒

HTTPS ≠ Safe

HTTPS only means the connection is encrypted — it does NOT guarantee the site is legitimate. Phishing sites regularly use HTTPS with valid certificates to appear trustworthy.
📌

Step-by-Step Instructions

  • 1
    Read each URL in the table below carefully
    Examine every part: protocol (http/https), domain, subdomains, path, and query parameters. Do not click any of these links.
  • 2
    Classify: Legitimate / Suspicious / Malicious
    Legitimate: Official domain, no anomalies. Suspicious: Unusual subdomain or path, needs verification. Malicious: Obvious fake domain, typosquatting, or known attack pattern.
  • 3
    Identify the specific attack technique
    Name the technique: typosquatting, subdomain abuse, HTTP (not HTTPS), URL shortener, misleading path, or homograph attack.
  • 4
    Scan 3 URLs on VirusTotal
    Go to virustotal.com → Search → paste the URL → note how many engines flag it. Screenshot your results.
  • 5
    Write one sentence explaining why each suspicious URL is dangerous
    In your worksheet: "This URL is dangerous because ___."
🔗

📊 Lab 2 — Debrief & Analysis

Target: Correctly classify 12 out of 15 URLs.

🔑 Key Rule

The REAL domain is always the part immediately before the TLD (.com, .net, .co.uk). Everything to the left is a subdomain.

⚠️ Common Mistake

People trust URLs because they see a familiar brand name — but the brand name might just be a subdomain of a malicious site.

🛠 Tools to Use

VirusTotal.com · URLScan.io · Whois Lookup · PhishTank · Google Safe Browsing Checker

Part B — MFA Setup & Login Simulation

ℹ️ What is MFA? Multi-Factor Authentication adds a second verification step beyond your password — something you HAVE (your phone) in addition to something you KNOW (password).
  • 1
    Install Google Authenticator on your phone
    Available free on iOS App Store and Google Play. Alternatively, use Microsoft Authenticator.
  • 2
    Open the demo account setup page provided by your trainer
    The trainer will provide a URL and QR code for a demo account with MFA enabled.
  • 3
    Scan the QR code with Google Authenticator
    Open Authenticator → Tap "+" → "Scan a QR Code" → Scan the trainer's code. A new entry appears with a 6-digit code.
  • 4
    Log in using: Username + Password + TOTP Code
    Enter your credentials + the current 6-digit code from the Authenticator app before it expires (30-second window).
  • 5
    Observe: try logging in with an expired or wrong code
    See how the system blocks access even if username and password are correct. This is MFA working correctly.
  • 6
    Discuss: what if your phone is stolen?
    Answer: Most MFA systems provide backup codes at setup. Always save them. Alternatively, use hardware security keys (YubiKey) for highest security.

LAB4

Phishing Simulation Response Drill

Practice the full 5-step response to a realistic phishing email. Draft an incident report, conduct a verification role-play, and debrief.

⏱ 25 minutes Advanced

📋 Scenario Brief

You arrive at work on Monday morning and find the following email in your inbox. Your task is to apply the 5-step action plan and respond correctly. Read the full email carefully before proceeding.

From:it.support@your-company.helpdesk-portal.com To:all.staff@yourcompany.com Date:Monday, 20 May 2025, 08:47 AM
🔒 [IT SECURITY NOTICE] Mandatory Password Reset Required — Complete Within 2 Hours
Dear Employee, Our security monitoring system has detected that your account password may have been exposed in a recent credential leak affecting several companies in your industry. To protect company data, you are required to reset your password IMMEDIATELY by clicking the link below. Failure to do so within 2 hours will result in your account being locked and you will need to contact HR to regain access. RESET YOUR PASSWORD NOW: http://yourcompany-secureportal.com/reset?token=emp_28471&redirect=internal Please also verify your identity by entering your: - Current username - Current password - Employee ID number - Date of birth This process will take less than 2 minutes. If you have any questions, do NOT reply to this email — instead click the link above where our team is available via live chat. Kind Regards, James Wilson IT Security Manager YourCompany IT Department Tel: +44 7891 234567
⚑ Analyse this email carefully ⚑ Apply the 5-step plan ⚑ Count the red flags
📋

Step 1: Apply the 5-Step Action Plan

🛑 STOP: Do NOT click any links, open any attachments, or reply to this email. Complete this analysis first.
  • 1
    STOP — Do not interact
    Close the email. Do not click any links, enter any information, or forward it to colleagues (except to IT Security). ✅ Your first action.
  • 2
    EXAMINE — Complete the red flag analysis below
    Work through the analysis checklist in Step 2 to catalogue every suspicious element.
  • 3
    VERIFY — Call IT Support on the KNOWN number
    Look up the real IT support number in the company directory (not the number in the email). Call and ask: "Did you send an all-staff password reset email this morning?"
  • 4
    REPORT — Submit an incident report
    Use the incident report form below to document and report to the security team. This helps protect your entire organisation.
  • 5
    DELETE — Remove from inbox and empty trash
    After reporting, delete the email from your inbox. Then empty your Deleted Items/Trash to prevent accidental clicking later.
🔍

Step 2: Red Flag Analysis Worksheet

Identify every red flag in the scenario email above. Tick each one you find, then reveal the full answer key.

  • Sender domain: your-company.helpdesk-portal.com — not yourcompany.com
  • Link domain: yourcompany-secureportal.com — not the real company site
  • Urgency: "within 2 hours" with threat of account lockout
  • Fear tactic: "password may have been exposed in a credential leak"
  • Requests current password: never share an existing password — reset links don't need it
  • Requests Date of Birth: sensitive PII not needed for a password reset
  • Prevents verification: "Do NOT reply to this email" removes an easy verification method
  • Sent to all-staff: mass blast, not targeted — a real IT notice would be via the intranet
  • HTTP link: http:// not https:// in the reset URL
  • Token in URL: ?token=emp_28471 — designed to harvest employee IDs
VERDICT: PHISHING — Score 10/10 red flags

Critical flags:
1. Sender domain — your-company.helpdesk-portal.com: "your-company" is a SUBDOMAIN of attacker's helpdesk-portal.com
2. Link domain — yourcompany-secureportal.com: NOT yourcompany.com
3. Requests current password — NEVER provide your current password in a "reset" form
4. Requests DOB — excessive personal data for a simple password reset

Psychological tactics used:
· URGENCY — 2-hour deadline
· FEAR — threat of account lockout
· AUTHORITY — impersonating IT Security Manager "James Wilson"
· PREVENTION OF VERIFICATION — "Do NOT reply" blocks easy verification

CORRECT ACTION: DO NOT click. Call IT on the known directory number. Report and delete.

Step 3 (Role-Play): Verification Call to IT Support

One participant plays the employee, another plays an IT Support agent. The employee should call to verify whether the password reset email was legitimate.

Employee Script

"Hi, this is [name] from [department]. I received an email this morning from someone claiming to be the IT Security Manager asking all staff to reset their passwords urgently. I wanted to verify whether this is genuine before doing anything.

The sender address was 'it.support@your-company.helpdesk-portal.com'. Can you confirm whether you sent this email?"

IT Support Script (Trainer)

"Thank you for calling to verify — that is exactly the right thing to do!

No, we did not send that email. The domain 'helpdesk-portal.com' is not our company domain. That is a phishing attempt.

Please do not click any links, report it to security@company.com, and delete it from your inbox. Well done for checking!"
✅ Key learning: Calling to verify is ALWAYS the right action. A real IT team will NEVER be annoyed by a verification call — they will be pleased you checked.

📊 Lab 4 — Final Debrief & Group Discussion

Success Criteria: Identify all red flags + complete the incident report + complete the verification role-play.

🔴 If You HAD Clicked the Link

  1. Disconnect from the network immediately (if possible)
  2. Call IT Security urgently: ext. 1234
  3. Do NOT try to undo or reverse anything yourself
  4. Change your password from a DIFFERENT, clean device
  5. Report to your line manager and complete an incident form
  6. IT will scan your device and check for malware or data exposure

🟢 What You Did Correctly

  1. Paused and did not react impulsively to urgency
  2. Examined the sender domain carefully
  3. Identified multiple red flags systematically
  4. Verified through an independent channel (phone call)
  5. Completed a detailed incident report
  6. Deleted the email after reporting

  • Which red flag was most convincing to you — why?
